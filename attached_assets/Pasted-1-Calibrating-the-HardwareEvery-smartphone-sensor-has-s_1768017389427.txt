1. Calibrating the HardwareEvery smartphone sensor has slight manufacturing variances. Calibration aligns the "digital" reality with the physical one.Accelerometer Calibration (The "6-Face" Method)The accelerometer measures the force of gravity ($9.81 \, m/s^2$). To calibrate it, you need to find the offset (bias) and scale factor.The Process: Ask the user (or your test rig) to place the phone perfectly flat on all six sides (screen up, screen down, left side, right side, top, bottom).The Goal: In a perfect world, the axis pointing up should read exactly $1g$ and the others $0g$. If it reads $1.05g$, you have a $5\%$ scale error to correct in your code.Gyroscope Calibration (Zero-Rate Offset)Gyroscopes suffer from "drift," where they think the phone is rotating even when it's sitting still.The Process: While the device is perfectly still, take an average of the readings over 2–3 seconds.The Goal: Subtract this average "bias" from all future readings to ensure the device stays at a true zero when stationary.2. Signal Processing: Cleaning the NoiseRaw sensor data is "jittery." If you use raw data, a person sitting on a vibrating bus will register thousands of steps.Apply a Low-Pass FilterA low-pass filter smooths out high-frequency noise (small jitters) and keeps the low-frequency signals (the actual movement of a human body).Pro-tip: Most human walking occurs at a frequency of 1Hz to 3Hz. Any signal significantly faster than that is likely noise.Use a Complementary or Kalman FilterIf you are using both the Accelerometer and the Gyroscope, you need to fuse them.Accelerometer: Great for long-term orientation but noisy in the short term.Gyroscope: Very accurate in the short term but drifts over time.The Fix: Use a Kalman Filter to weigh the strengths of both, providing a steady, "grounded" estimate of the device's movement.3. The Step Detection AlgorithmOnce the data is clean, you need to identify what actually constitutes a "step."FeatureDescriptionMagnitude CalculationInstead of looking at X, Y, or Z individually, calculate the Vector Magnitude: $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$. This makes the app work regardless of how the user holds the phone.Dynamic ThresholdingDon't use a fixed "peak." A jogger has a much harder impact than a slow walker. Your algorithm should calculate a "moving average" threshold.Step TimingHumans can't physically take 10 steps in one second. Set a timing window (e.g., 200ms to 2000ms) between peaks to filter out false positives.4.
